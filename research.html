Research

Statistical and AI methods for biomedical research.

My research develops statistical and AI methods for biomedical data that are observational rather than experimental, often ordinal or mixed-typed, frequently distributed across institutions, and drawn from complex surveys or unstructured clinical text. The program organizes around three thrusts that share methodological foundations but address distinct questions in biostatistics, artificial intelligence, and health services research.

i

Statistical methods for complex biomedical data

Most biomedical data violate the assumptions of classical causal inference, arriving as mixed-typed variables, weighted survey samples, ordinal ratings, or records split across institutions. I work on the following projects to address these challenges.

  • Information-theoretic measures of asymmetric association that recover causal direction from observational data, with Peter X.-K. Song.
  • Minimum Bregman divergence inference for robust estimation, with Ayanendranath Basu.
  • Ordinal Bayesian network learning that handles survey weights and covariates jointly, with David Frank, Lauren Russell, and Leslie Hausmann.
  • Federated estimating equations for inference across multicenter cohorts without pooling individual-level data.

ii

AI models for medical device safety

With colleagues at VA Pittsburgh, I co-develop AI models for adverse-event surveillance. Our current focus is insulin pumps and continuous glucose monitors — the second-largest source of medical-device-related injuries reported to the FDA. Active extensions integrate extracted risk signal into provider-facing registries and Veteran-facing safety education materials. My collaborators include:

  • Jamie Estock (Human Factors)
  • Ronald Codario (Endocrinology)

iii

Health services research (HSR)

I serve as the statistician on a portfolio of health services and clinical research projects, most of them at VA Pittsburgh's Center for Healthcare Evaluation, Research, and Promotion (CHERP).

Active projects span six areas:

  • Medication initiation and persistence, with Tim Anderson and Utibe Essien
  • Social determinants and health outcomes, with Leslie Hausmann
  • Cancer prevention, with José Zevallos
  • Pharmacogenomic implementation, with Christine Kistler
  • Sarcoidosis and military exposures, with Mohamed Seedahmed
  • Transplantation and surgical outcomes, with Vrishketan Sethi
